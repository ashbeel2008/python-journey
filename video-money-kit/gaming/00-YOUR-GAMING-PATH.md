## Your laptop decides your lane (read this twice)

Your machine: **i5-4210U (2 cores), 6 GB RAM, Intel HD graphics, 59 GB free.**

That is a light laptop. It can absolutely edit **30–60 second vertical clips**. It will hate
long-form, 4K, and 60fps timelines.

So your lane is: **short-form gaming clips.**

Which happens to be the fastest lane to money anyway. Convenient.

### Rules for your machine

- You already have the right stack: **Lunar Rewind** to record, **Premiere Pro 2019** to edit.
  Do not add DaVinci Resolve or heavy plugins — 6 GB RAM is the hard limit.
- Edit at **1080p, 30fps**. Never 4K. Avoid 60fps timelines.
- Set Premiere playback resolution to **1/4 or 1/8** while editing. Export at 1080p.
- Download only the **clip segment** you need — never drag a 6-hour VOD into the timeline.
- Keep at least **20 GB free**. Delete exports after delivery.
- Close your browser while exporting.
- Follow `minecraft/04-PREMIERE-LOW-END.md`. It is the difference between working and freezing.

## Who actually pays (and who wastes your time)

**Target these:**
- Streamers with **100–2,000 concurrent viewers** who are monetized (subs, donations, sponsors).
  They stream 3–5×/week, drown in footage, and need clips constantly.
- Gaming YouTubers with **5k–100k subscribers** posting inconsistently.
- Small gaming brands / energy drink / peripheral promos — later, once you have proof.

**Avoid these:**
- 0–20 viewer streamers with no income. They will offer "revenue share" and vanish.
- "We're about to blow up, edit for free and we'll bring you with us."
- Anyone who says "we'll pay you when the channel monetizes."

## 30-day skill ladder (45 min editing + 15 min learning per day)

### Week 1 — Mechanics
- Premiere interface: timeline, razor (`Ctrl+K`), ripple delete, speed/duration, keyframes
- Auto-captions + gaming caption styles
- 3–4 sound effects you'll reuse forever (whoosh, boom, vine boom, pop)
- **Deliverable:** one 20–30s clip, start to finish

### Week 2 — Structure
- The 3-second hook rule: context + payoff promised immediately
- Cut every dead frame. Gaming clips die in silence.
- J-cuts and L-cuts (audio from the next scene starts before the visual)
- Audio ducking: game audio down, voice/commentary up
- **Deliverable:** 3 clips from one VOD

### Week 3 — Retention
- Pattern interrupts every 3–5 seconds (zoom, text, sfx, meme overlay)
- Zoom punch-ins on reaction moments
- Loop the ending back to the start
- **Deliverable:** 5 clips, one of each style

### Week 4 — Batch and polish
- Build a reusable Premiere project template (captions, fonts, sfx, safe zones)
- Thumbnails / cover frames
- Export settings + delivery naming
- **Deliverable:** 10-clip pack, consistent style

**Learning rule:** max 15 minutes of tutorials per day. The other 45 is editing. Watching is not
practice.

## Your 3 portfolio pieces (this is your proof)

Make these three. They show range and take one evening each.

1. **Funny moment** — comedic timing, meme captions, sound effects.
2. **Insane play / highlight** — beat sync, slow-mo, impact, clean text.
3. **Commentary / talking clip** — subtitles, zoom cuts, b-roll.

### Where to get footage (you don't need a gaming PC)

1. **Your own gameplay** — phone games, older/indie games, anything that runs.
2. **Ask small streamers for permission** — "Can I cut a 20-second sample from your VOD for my
   portfolio? I'll send it to you free." Most say yes. **This doubles as outreach.**
3. **No-copyright gameplay** for practice only — never for paid client work.

# 02 — Pricing and Offer

Prices below are **starting points in USD** for a beginner with 0–3 testimonials.
Convert to your local currency if you're selling locally. International clients pay in USD.

> These are not guarantees and not "market rates." They are deliberately low enough to get your
> first yes, high enough that you're not working for free.

---

## Starting prices

| Service | Beginner price | Notes |
|---------|----------------|-------|
| 1 short-form video (30–60s) | $30–50 | Captions + licensed music + 2 revisions |
| 10-clip podcast pack | $150–250 | One long episode → 10 clips |
| Local business promo (client footage) | $150–300 | Script + edit, no shooting |
| Thumbnail | $10–20 | Pairs well with video packages |
| Long-form YouTube edit (5–15 min) | $80–200 | Higher effort, slower to learn |

**Rule: quote per project or per package. Never per hour.** Per-hour punishes you for getting fast.

---

## Your three packages

Use the same structure for every niche. Adjust the numbers only.

### Starter — $180/month
- 4 short-form videos (1/week)
- Captions, licensed music, basic color
- 1 revision round per video
- 5-business-day turnaround per video
- *Best for: first-time clients testing you*

### Growth — $450/month  ← push this one
- 12 short-form videos (3/week)
- Captions, music, hooks written for you
- Thumbnail/cover frame
- 2 revision rounds per video
- 3-business-day turnaround
- Monthly performance notes (what to post when)

### Pro — $750/month
- 20 short-form videos + 4 thumbnails
- Content calendar + hook bank
- 2 revision rounds
- 48-hour turnaround
- Monthly 20-minute strategy call

**Always show three options.** Most people pick the middle. Design the middle to be the one you
want to sell.

---

## Scope boundaries (write these into every deal)

Include:
- [ ] Exact number of videos
- [ ] Length range (e.g., 30–60 seconds)
- [ ] Format (1080×1920 vertical, MP4)
- [ ] Captions: burned-in or .srt
- [ ] Revision rounds: **2**
- [ ] Turnaround time
- [ ] Delivery method (Google Drive link)

Exclude (say this out loud):
- Raw project files / editing project file
- Unlimited revisions
- Scriptwriting from scratch (unless in the package)
- Shooting / travel
- Posting to their accounts
- Paid stock footage or music subscriptions

---

## Payment terms

1. **50% deposit** before editing starts. Non-negotiable.
2. **50% on delivery**, before you send the final unwatermarked files.
3. Retainers: **paid upfront monthly**, on the 1st, before work begins.
4. Late by 7 days → work pauses. Late by 14 days → 5% late fee (state it in the contract).
5. Watermark previews until final payment clears.
6. Add a **rush fee of 30%** for under-48-hour turnaround.

---

## The price ladder

This is how you stop being cheap without losing clients:

1. Land 2–3 clients at starter prices.
2. Collect a testimonial from each.
3. **Raise your price 20–30%.**
4. Repeat until you start losing ~1 in 3 deals. That's roughly your market rate.
5. Never lower your price to win a deal. Offer a smaller package instead.

Example: $30/clip → $40 → $50 → $65 → $85 over a year, as proof accumulates.

---

## Free work policy

Free work is allowed **only** in these two forms:

1. **Spec samples you choose.** You pick a public creator/business, make one 20–30-second sample,
   and send it. They didn't ask; there's no obligation. Good for starting conversations.
2. **A watermarked 15–20-second sample** for a serious prospect who asked for proof. Timebox it
   to 45 minutes.

Never do a full free edit "as a test." Never work "for exposure." Never do "the first one free."

---

## What to say when they ask "how much?"

> "For [X videos] per month it's [$Y], paid monthly. That includes captions, music, [Z] revisions,
> and delivery in [N] days. If you want to start smaller, I can do a 4-video starter pack for [$A]."

Give a number. Silence after. Don't negotiate against yourself before they respond.

---

## Checkpoint

You are done when you have:
- [ ] Three packages with prices written down
- [ ] Two revision rounds in your scope
- [ ] A 50% deposit rule
- [ ] One sentence for "how much?"
